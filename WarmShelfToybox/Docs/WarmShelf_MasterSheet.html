Warm Shelf Master Sheet

Calm digital toys that feel alive in a toddler's hands.

Warm Shelf is an iPad-first collection of tactile digital toys for children ages 2 to 4. The product is not a game, not a lesson, and not a reward loop. It is a warm shelf of living objects that invite touch, care, repetition, and discovery.

App: WarmShelfToybox Platform: iOS, iPad-first Engine: Swift, UIKit, SpriteKit Current toys: Bubbles, Clay Blocks, Feed the People Next focus: sensory polish and parent area

North Star

Hand the app silently to a 2-year-old and see whether they reach for it. Every interaction should answer immediately, then settle gently. The child should never need to read, win, understand a menu, or chase a score. The product succeeds when a small human touches the screen because the toy feels present.

Current State

The project has moved from a single vertical slice into an early two-toy platform spine. The architecture is intentionally small: reusable scene foundations, shared motion and physics helpers, and one registry for future toys.

Non-Negotiable Product Rules

Inside Toys

  • No child-facing text.
  • No instructions.
  • No scores, levels, stars, streaks, win states, or fail states.
  • No ads, accounts, analytics, onboarding, or monetization in this slice.
  • No dead taps. Empty space must still answer softly.
  • One satisfying feedback moment at a time.

Feel

  • Immediate response within calm.
  • Slow drift, gentle settling, soft easing.
  • Tactile, hand-touched, warm, low-stimulation visuals.
  • No neon, harsh outlines, arcade UI, glossy mobile-game language, or chaos loops.
  • Every scene should feel alive before touch.

Three-Toy System

The platform should cover three developmental modes: sensory repetition, physical cause and effect, and emotional relational play.

Bubbles

Sensory and repetition. Tap to pop. Soft drift, visible bubbles, gentle particle burst, no score.

Clay Blocks

Physical and causal. Drag, stack, tumble, build upward. A quiet mystery tray gives more shapes.

Feed the People

Emotional and relational. Feed simple characters, receive warm expressions, reset calmly. First playable slice is now in the app.

Architecture

Shared Foundation

  • BaseToyScene: reusable SpriteKit scene base, paper background, motes, return handle.
  • ToyRegistry: single registration point for shelf toys.
  • ToyViewController: presents any registered toy scene.
  • ToyShelfScene: living shelf rendered from the registry.
  • WarmShelfPalette: brand color source of truth.

Interaction Foundation

  • ToyPhysicsProfile: shared touch and motion profiles.
  • ForgivingHitArea: generous toddler touch targeting.
  • TouchFeedbackAnimator: immediate acknowledgement and soft visual feedback.
  • ParticleManager: soft bursts, motes, and ripples.
  • AudioManager and HapticsManager: sensory layer.

Apple Design Award Lens

The app is strong in philosophy, architecture, and interaction values. Submission readiness depends on sensory craft, accessibility, trust, and a complete three-toy product shape.

Strengths

  • Clear product values expressed in code.
  • Authored physics rather than raw simulation everywhere.
  • Non-numeric memory instead of score.
  • Real toddler-test driven changes.
  • Calm visual identity with a real palette system.

Hard Gaps

  • Handcrafted sound library is still missing.
  • Haptics need physical-device testing, especially iPhone versus iPad behavior.
  • Accessibility foundation needs verification with VoiceOver.
  • Parent area and trust screen are not built.
  • Third toy is not yet implemented.

Next Objectives

  1. Finish the sensory pass: bubble pop variants, empty-tap audio, block pickup/release/settle, mystery tray sound, shelf transition sound, and real-world volume testing.
  2. Verify haptics on device. Treat haptics as core on iPhone and graceful no-op on iPad hardware that does not support Taptic feedback.
  3. Build a minimal parent area behind a deliberate adult gesture. Include no ads, no accounts, no data collection, how to return to shelf, and contact/support.
  4. Toddler-test Feed the People with three characters, five food items, generous snap zones, simple expression states, and no text.
  5. Re-test shelf with three toys. The test is whether each toy earns a first reach without labels.

Testing Checklist

Toddler Test

  • Does the child reach within five seconds?
  • Does the first tap produce visible understanding?
  • Do they repeat the action without prompting?
  • Do they discover the mystery tray naturally?
  • Does the block camera follow feel like building upward, not a moving camera?

Parent and Product Test

  • Can a parent find return-to-shelf without making it a child-facing button?
  • Does the app explain trust without cluttering the child experience?
  • Does VoiceOver expose meaningful areas?
  • Do sounds feel soft on real iPad speakers?
  • Do portrait and landscape preserve play continuity?

Not Now

These are intentionally excluded until the spine, sensory layer, trust layer, and three-toy v1 pitch are strong.

No monetization.
No accounts.
No analytics.
No onboarding.
No settings panel for children.
No Garage until Bubbles, Blocks, and Feed prove the platform.
