# Build 5: design review through an awards lens

This is our design assessment, not an Apple jury decision. It uses the founder’s build 4 feedback and the revised source. The founder owns the new build’s visual, listening and interaction review. SDK and helper checks cannot establish a child’s understanding or enjoyment.

Apple’s current award categories include Delight and Fun, Inclusivity, Innovation, Interaction, Social Impact, and Visuals and Graphics. Lull’s most credible direction is quiet physical interaction and delight: a box that returns the same treasures, singing keys, responsive felt stones and a garden that follows a child. [Apple Design Awards](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/)

## Strongest elements

- Sleepy Box has a clear, self-correcting shape fit and visible object return. The new box changes position without changing the shape rule.
- Hum and Meadow offer open exploration with no score or prescribed winning sequence. Their feedback should make a child’s action easy to understand.
- The parent onboarding already explains nine toys for ages 2–6, choosing and repeating through pictures/touch, local family settings, and the evening routine. The parent purchase page explains the full toybox and uses Apple’s localized price with restore support. These are concrete benefits; they avoid unsupported claims about educational outcomes.

## What still separates this from an exceptional release

- Material consistency needs a founder visual pass, particularly Mix-Up’s returning characters, Feed’s current three illustrated friends and the felt stone rendering. This pass adds future cast integration and art prompts; it does not invent a claim that new diversity art has shipped.
- Interaction must feel reliable in landscape on real phones and iPads: no misplaced wish bubbles, missed food edges, floating dots or confused supply refills. The review guide concentrates on those known failures.
- Inclusivity needs actual device review of VoiceOver, Reduce Motion, different motor abilities and sound preferences. Colour is never the rule for accepting a dot. A wider illustrated cast remains an art follow-up.
- Innovation is currently the weakest awards argument: familiar toy verbs alone are common. The consistent sound/material language, gentle household rhythm and the child’s sense of agency need to form a memorable whole. More effects alone will not achieve that.
- Conversion depends on the free experience being worth returning to and the parent offer being trustworthy. Confirm the trial policy, genuine purchase/restore behavior and product metadata before public release. Avoid child-facing sales pressure.

## Release decision

Aim for an excellent, dependable first release. Do not label it award-ready from source checks. The next concrete gate is the founder’s build 5 device approval; public release also needs the outstanding support/privacy website, store screenshots, purchase setup and Kids age-band decisions in the handoff. Montessori-informed activity rationale should be reviewed by an early-childhood educator before making educational claims.
