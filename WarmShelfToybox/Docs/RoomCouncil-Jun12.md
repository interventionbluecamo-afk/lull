# The Room Council — June 12
*The founder asked: he wants to MOVE things — the cat, the plant, the lamps. New
items in the room every visit. Maybe a kitchen. "This is a room." The council
convened: the feature-animation veteran, the chart-topping kids-iOS pro, the
Montessori doctor, the engineer, and the jury chair. Considered deeply, with
lived experience. Verdict at the end.*

---

## The animation veteran (DreamWorks/Pixar, 20 years of believable rooms)

"A room becomes real the moment objects remember where they were. Not physics —
*belonging*. In feature work we never let a hero prop float free; it has a home,
a path between homes, and a settle animation that says 'I live here.' Your lean
already gives the room dimension. What it needs is **object permanence with
consent**: the child picks the cat up, the cat goes soft (we have squash, we have
purrs), and the room offers it two or three *seats* — the sill, the rug, the
armchair when it comes. Free-drag-anywhere reads as chaos and breaks the diorama.
Slot-to-slot reads as care. And one more law from the lighting floor: when an
object moves, its light must move — the cat on the rug at night should catch the
night light. You already run a household clock; let it touch everything that moves."

## The chart-topping kids-iOS pro (shipped two Kids-Category #1s; Pok Pok envy and scars)

"Pok Pok's rooms work because of three disciplines, and people copy the wrong one.
1) **Density is capped** — five to seven touchables per screen, every one with a
real response. 2) **Novelty is a drip, not a flood** — their 'new thing' cadence
is weekly-ish and items *return*; kids greet them like friends. A new item every
visit trains checking behavior — that's a slot machine in felt clothing, and
review teams notice. 3) **Verbs over objects** — a watering can that works beats
ten decorations. You already LANDED the watering can, the pot's three growth
stages, and a suncatcher. Wire the verb before you buy more nouns.
The kitchen? That's not a feature, that's a SECOND ROOM — a 1.1 flagship, not a
pre-submission scramble. Ship one deep room; tease the doorway."

## The Montessori doctor

"A prepared environment is loved *because* it is stable. The child's power comes
from mastering where things live — moving the cat to the rug is agency; finding
a stranger on the sill every morning is weather. So: yes to movable, with homes.
Yes to caring verbs — watering is the single best one (responsibility, delayed
result, real-clock growth: water today, the bud opens tomorrow — that is a
Montessori lesson in patience wearing a felt costume). To 'new every visit' I
say: **rotate, never accumulate** — a small basket of familiar visitors, one out
at a time. And the child must never be punished for moving things: no wrong
placements, no resets. The room remembers what they did. That memory IS the toy."

## The engineer (me, who has to ship this)

"Inventory check: we already have pet-the-cat, shiver-the-plant, two light
switches, the lean with three depths, and three UNWIRED assets (can, pot stages
1–3, suncatcher). The lean already persists; persistence of *placements* is one
small UserDefaults dictionary. Slot-based placement is a weekend, not a month:
each movable gets 2–3 anchor points, drag = lift + soft carry + snap with the
house feel verbs we already have everywhere else. Real-clock pot growth rides
TimeOfDay, which every scene already reads. The visit-gift needs a date check and
an authored set — start with the suncatcher we own. The kitchen needs a camera
system we don't have; correctly priced as post-launch."

## The jury chair

"The window with a watering can the child must remember to use, a cat that can be
*put to bed* on the rug, and a pot that answers tomorrow — that's an editorial
story. 'A hundred objects' is not. Depth wins awards; volume wins nothing."

---

## VERDICT — unanimous

**Yes to the living room. No to the infinite room. Kitchen later.**

### Phase A — pre-submission (wire what we own, ~1–2 sessions)
1. **The watering verb** ⭐ — can lives on the sill; drag it over the pot, it
   tips and sprinkles (felt-drop particles, soft tone); the pot advances
   sprout → bud → bloom across REAL days (one stage per watered day; the
   household clock teaches patience). Assets: LANDED.
2. **Movable with homes** — cat: sill ↔ rug (asleep on rug at night = the
   night-light catches her). Plant: sill ↔ floor corner. Lamp stays (it's
   plugged in — the animator insists, and it's funnier). Lift/carry/settle
   with existing squash + purr + shiver verbs. Placements persist.
3. **The visiting things** — ONE guest object per day's first visit, from a
   rotating authored basket, placed in a fixed guest spot: suncatcher (owned),
   then 3–4 generated: a paper boat, a wooden duck, a ball of yarn, a tiny
   book. Returning guests, never strangers forever.

### Phase B — post-launch 1.1 (the doorway)
- The kitchen as a second viewport reached by leaning PAST the rubber band at
  the room's edge (the lean already wants this); two or three caring verbs
  (kettle that whistles softly, bread that toasts by the clock, a bowl for the
  cat — which connects the rooms: carry her food over).
- Seasonal guests in the basket.

### Phase A imagegen seeds (full prompts on request, house rules apply)
- `window-guest-boat` / `window-guest-duck` / `window-guest-yarn` /
  `window-guest-book` — small sill-scale objects, side view, square.
- `window-cat-rug-asleep` — the cat curled on the round rug, top-lit for night.
- (Kitchen sheet — Phase B, not now.)

*— adjourned. The watering can goes first; it was always going to be the
watering can.*
