# Lull — Brand

## The name
**Lull** (verb): to soothe, to calm, to quiet toward rest. The whole product is the verb made
real — including the room that warms and dims toward bedtime.

## One line
**Calm toys, made for little hands.**

(App Store subtitle candidate: *"A gentle toybox for ages 2–4. No ads, no scores — just play."*)

## What Lull is
A small, beautiful shelf of living digital toys for toddlers and preschoolers. Each toy is a
self-contained world that responds to touch like a creature, not a button. Open-ended. No goals,
no timers, no winning. A digital version of a well-loved wooden toybox.

## What Lull is not
Not a learning app with quizzes. Not a reward loop. Not loud, flashing, or busy. Not ad-supported.
Not a subscription. Children never see a price, a lock, or a streak.

## Voice
- **To the child:** silent. No text, no narration, no instructions. The toys speak in motion and sound.
- **To the parent:** warm, plain, and quietly confident. Short sentences. No hype, no jargon, no
  guilt. We say what we don't do ("no ads, ever") as much as what we do.
- Words we use: calm, soft, gentle, living, hush, wind-down, little hands.
- Words we avoid: educational, level, unlock (to kids), best, #1, addictive, engaging.

## Color story
Warm linen paper and clay. Sun-faded, hand-dyed, never neon. The palette feels like afternoon
light on a wooden shelf. Accents (terracotta, sage, butter, lavender, water-blue, petal) are the
toys themselves. Ink is cocoa/clay, never pure black. See `WarmShelfPalette`.

## The mark
The signature sleepy stacking friend on warm linen: terracotta clay, low closed eyes, rosy cheeks,
a cream moon patch, and one tiny butter-clay capstone balanced on its head. The capstone makes the
play mechanic readable without using score, achievement, or crown imagery. Text-free as an icon.
The wordmark is "Lull" set in Georgia, lowercase warmth, on a soft tinted disc.

Canonical implementation, 2026-06-07: the capstone is the only Lull host mark in code,
onboarding, app icons, and brand-hero art. Crown-coded shapes may appear only as in-world
king/queen play-character accessories when they serve toddler recognition; they are never
the product mark.

## Signature feelings (what every surface should evoke)
1. **Noticed** — the world reacts to the child instantly and kindly. (See `ExpressionPrinciples.md`.)
2. **Safe** — a parent relaxes the moment they see it. The promise screen exists for this.
3. **Hush** — it gets cozier toward bedtime, on the family's own schedule.

## Marketing in one beat (15s)
A toddler's finger lifts a sleepy stone; it gasps awake and beams; the tower grows and the top
stone yawns and glows. No UI, no text — just the toy feeling something. That's the ad.
