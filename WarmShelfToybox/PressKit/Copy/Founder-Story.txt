LULL — FOUNDER STORY NOTES
Use as a story outline, not as a verbatim founder quote.

A parent is building a quiet digital toybox, then learning from watching his own son use it. Meadow and Little Wash have been particular favorites. Requests for more vehicles, reactions to sound, and the way a small hand drags across the screen have helped shape the work.

The story is not “we got every detail right at once.” It is about making a collection that feels cared for: noticing what a child enjoys repeating, taking awkward moments seriously, and building parent controls into the experience rather than treating them as an afterthought.

Useful interview questions:
1. What did your son do in Lull that surprised you?
2. Why do you think he comes back to Meadow and Little Wash?
3. What changed after you watched him try to pick up a tool or shape?
4. How did you decide what the toybox should do when playtime ends?
5. What does “calm” mean in the details of sound, movement and repetition?
6. What are you still learning from family testing?

Do not invent answers. The founder can record responses in his own words; that recording can become another NotebookLM source.

Do not publish the child's name, face, location or a fabricated quote from this file. This kit contains no child photographs. Add only family media the founder deliberately selects for publication.
