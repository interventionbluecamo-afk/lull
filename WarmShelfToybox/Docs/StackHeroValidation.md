# Stack Hero Validation

## Runway Rule

Do not fund more launch-toy breadth or paid acquisition until Stack proves that children
understand the interaction and parents recognize the value.

Why: one excellent, observed play loop is a safer foundation than six polished guesses.

## Low-Cost Test

Test with five children ages 2-4, with a parent present and consenting. Use both an iPhone
and iPad if available. Do not explain the toy, point at the screen, collect child names,
or record identifying video.

Set device volume near 50%. Start on the child shelf. Let each child play for up to ten
minutes while the adult quietly records only anonymous counts and observations.

## Child Proof

Stack is ready to lead the product when:

- At least 4 of 5 children touch Stack from the shelf without adult prompting.
- At least 4 of 5 lift and release a stone within 30 seconds.
- At least 4 of 5 voluntarily repeat a stack, drop, or topple loop three times.
- At least 4 of 5 re-engage after a tower falls instead of treating it as failure.
- No child needs an explanation to continue.
- No child repeatedly loses stones below the floor, outside reach, or behind another
  object.
- No child repeatedly taps decorative room details expecting a missing interaction.
- The shelf-return handle is discoverable by the parent but not triggered accidentally
  by the child during play.

## Parent Proof

After play, ask the parent:

1. "What do you think your child was enjoying?"
2. "What feels different from other kids apps?"
3. "What would make you comfortable paying once for the full toybox?"
4. "At what one-time price would this feel inexpensive, reasonable, expensive, and too
   expensive?"

Do not lead with the current price. Record anonymous phrases and price ranges.

Stack is ready to support a paid product when at least 3 of 5 parents independently name
the calm emotional response, lack of ads/pressure, or tactile quality as valuable.

## Decision Rules

- If first reach fails, strengthen the shelf silhouette and opening invitation.
- If dragging works but stacking fails, increase placement assistance and favor flatter
  stones.
- If toppling is repeated more than stacking, treat joyful collapse as a primary loop,
  not a mistake.
- If children stay but parents do not see value, improve authored art, sound, and the
  parent story before adding content.
- If neither children nor parents respond, stop expanding Lull and revisit the product
  premise before spending more.

## Session Debrief

After all five sessions, answer:

- How many children entered Stack without prompting?
- How many lifted a stone within 30 seconds?
- How many repeated a loop at least three times?
- How many recovered happily after a topple?
- What action caused the most repeated confusion?
- What exact value did parents name without prompting?
- Where does the median parent place a reasonable lifetime price?
- Does the evidence say proceed, fix one step, or stop expanding?
