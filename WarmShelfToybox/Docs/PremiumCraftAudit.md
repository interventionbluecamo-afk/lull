# Premium Craft Audit

## Honest State

Lull now has a coherent promise, a truthful free shelf, and a recognizable Stack hero.
It is not yet at top-charts or Apple-featured craft. The strongest current work is the
calm product philosophy and the Stack interaction. The largest remaining cap is that too
much of the toybox still looks and moves like well-tuned procedural SpriteKit rather than
an authored world with its own materials, acting language, and sound identity.

## What This Pass Fixed

- Stack now uses anticipation, follow-through, secondary capstone motion, cascading tower
  response, and staged light rather than relying on particles for emotion.
- Stack only wakes for a genuinely supported build. A piece released high in the air no
  longer earns the tower payoff.
- Stack pickup, appearance, settling, and waking have distinct sensory meanings.
- Bubbles now shows a pressure wave before nearby bubbles follow a pop, making the chain
  reaction legible as cause-and-effect.
- The shelf and its contact shadows stay grounded. Living toys move; furniture does not.
- Launch now centers the actual free-play promise: Bubbles and the signature Stack friend.
- Reduced Motion now receives a short fade instead of a large screen zoom.
- Parent copy now truthfully describes the always-available Bubbles and Stack shelf.

## What Still Caps Quality

### 1. Authored Materials

Most surfaces are layered vector shapes with procedural speckles. They are cohesive, but
they do not yet have the tactile specificity of clay, paper, wood, glass, and fabric made
by a top art team.

**Why:** Material recognition creates trust and sensory curiosity before a toddler
understands a mechanic.

### 2. Uneven Acting Across Toys

Stack now has an authored motion language. Feed, Bloom, Mix-Up, and Paint still contain
many generic wiggles, random rotations, and particle-led reactions.

**Why:** A premium toybox feels directed by one animation lead, not assembled from
individually acceptable scenes.

### 3. Bespoke Foley

Several interactions still depend on synthesized fallback tones or reused recordings.
Stack needs recorded soft-clay lift, soft-clay landing variants, and a quiet signature
wake sound mixed on real iPhone and iPad speakers.

**Why:** Tiny hands learn material cause-and-effect as much through sound as motion.

### 4. Observed Device QA

The current machine is missing the required iOS runtime, so the latest pass has source
verification but not eyes-on-device animation, speaker, haptic, frame-pacing, or toddler
reachability review.

**Why:** Animation timing that reads in code can still feel slow, loud, small, or busy in
a child's hand.

### 5. First-Session Proof

The free pair is strategically right, but the handoff from onboarding to shelf to first
successful Stack build has not been observed with families.

**Why:** Retention comes from an immediate feeling of competence and curiosity, not from
adding reward systems.

## Mechanics Sense Check

- **Bubbles:** Tap produces an immediate pop. Nearby follow-on pops now visibly receive
  the pressure wave first. Watch whether two automatic neighbors preserve enough agency
  for age two; reduce to one if children stop targeting bubbles themselves.
- **Stack:** Touch lifts exactly under the finger, artwork follows with soft lag, release
  receives forgiving center assistance, and only a supported tall build wakes the friend.
- **Shelf:** Every object visible to the child opens. Purchases, prices, and locks remain
  in the parent area.
- **No fail states:** There are no timers, scores, lives, penalties, or destructive reset
  loops in the free first-session path.

## Next Work Order

1. Run and record the first ten minutes on a physical iPhone and iPad, then tune timing,
   target sizes, volume, and visual density from observation.
2. Bring Bubbles to the same authored standard as Stack: surface deformation, quieter
   particle language, and a distinct mother-bubble performance.
3. Produce a small owned material and foley set for clay, bubble film, wood shelf, and
   paper air; replace the most visible procedural placeholders first.
4. Apply one acting law across Feed, Bloom, Mix-Up, and Paint: anticipation, caused
   response, follow-through, rest.
5. Test the free shelf and parent purchase story with families before spending on broader
   content or acquisition.

## Hard Stops

- No child-facing locks, prices, upgrade prompts, behavioral ads, streaks, or timed
  pressure.
- No hidden interaction whose main purpose is to prolong play rather than deepen play.
- No subscription or purchase claim presented as guaranteed revenue.
