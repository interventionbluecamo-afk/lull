# Awards-Table Review — June 11, evening
*Written as the room would write it: the App Store editorial/awards panel, deciding
whether Lull belongs on the Design Award shortlist. The Monument Valley and paper.io
hires weigh in on Meadow at the end. No grade inflation — the jury never inflates.*

---

## The one-paragraph case

A two-person team (and a 2.8-year-old test director) built a nine-toy toybox with no
ads, no scores, no fail states, no text aimed at the child, and a household clock that
runs through every room. The toys *settle* instead of celebrate. The objects are
finite and honest — what you drop comes back, what you pour returns, nothing fades
into nowhere. One toy plants spring with a snail. The craft discipline (one palette,
one paper grain, one light law, faces on everything) is the kind juries cite. It is
not finished — audio is the loudest silence in the app — but the shape of an
award-worthy thing is unmistakably here.

## Toy by toy, award lens

**Shelf Room — shortlist-ready.** Night mode with Wren dozing remains the single
best frame. Parallax breath gives it dimensional life; the carved nameplate and the
companion possessions read as a loved home, not a menu. *Remaining:* Wren's authored
art pose.

**Window — shortlist-ready, and quietly the deepest toy.** Real-clock sky, the
curtain peek-a-boo loop (object permanence as a self-invented game) is jury-bait in
the best sense. Cat awake/asleep finally legible. *Remaining:* frame-art calibration
session, the watering-can/pot/suncatcher verbs (assets queued) would make it the demo
toy.

**Sleepy Box — strong.** Honest pour, the composed lullaby (the box hums back the
child's own posting order) is the kind of detail awards essays get written about.
Star now turns to meet its tilted hole. *Remaining:* body-v2 art (gold star ring,
frontal square) queued; teal history fully cleaned.

**Stack — strong.** The felt-tint system is invisible craft: one stone art, every
color. The child owns the knockdown. No open items beyond the long-parked touch
report.

**Feed — one session from strong.** Counter staging works; the authored cast (20
expression cells) is landed but not yet wired — the balloon-head procedural cast is
the most visible spell-break left in the app. The sandwich ask gives it the toybox's
first compound request. *This is the highest-leverage remaining session.*

**Mix-Up — strong, getting stronger.** Twenty characters, theater staging, the bow.
The six new citizens (two skin tones the cast lacked, dancer, firefighter, officer,
alien, astronaut) close a representation gap a jury WILL notice — queued tonight.
*Remaining:* per-band registration sweep when the new sheets land; cubby selection
mechanic.

**Drop Dots — turned the corner today.** The felt pivot (dots wear the five ring
colors) gave the toy its missing logic — color affordance without color rules, honest
weight in sound, the golden dot, the truthful pour. *Remaining:* nothing structural;
polish only.

**Bubbles — better house, wrong sky.** The pot wiring works, pearls read beautifully
— but the founder is right that a 3D room behind 2D bubbles argues with itself. The
air-field backdrops (queued) resolve it: weather, not architecture.

**Hum — the one perspective crime.** Top-down bars on a front-view bed. The top-down
bed regen is queued; the staged prewarm fixed the cold start. Until the regen lands
this is the weakest frame in the app.

**Meadow — see the hired guns below.**

## The Monument Valley + paper.io hires on Meadow

**MV person:** "The bones are poetic — territory-as-gardening is the gentlest
possible reading of conquest mechanics. Today's light pass (the snail's carried
lantern, dew beads, seams that can't cross green) moved it from 'systems demo' toward
'place.' What it still needs to be a *postcard*: the daytime ground art (queued —
the procedural straw is serviceable, not transporting), distant landmark silhouettes
so the horizon promises something, and a palette grade per time-of-day rather than a
single multiply. Light should be the reward for everything."

**paper.io person:** "Flow is fixed. Speed + camera + smooth heading made it feel
alive; the auto-home close is the single most important change — in our game the
crash teaches the loop, here the snail teaches it. Watch-me on first open closes the
tutorial gap without a tutorial. Remaining flow debt: nothing blocking. If you want
depth later: a second wanderer (a beetle that walks the trail behind the snail) gives
co-op without UI."

**Both, jointly:** ship the day-ground art, then stop touching it until device
playtests say otherwise.

## What stands between this and the shortlist

1. **Audio (session 7, deliberately skipped) — the loudest gap.** Tones are lovely;
   ambience is absent. A jury plays with sound on.
2. **Feed cast wiring** — the last procedural faces in an authored world.
3. **Hum bed regen + Bubbles air plates** — the two perspective/coherence arguments.
4. **Parent area (session 11)** — the grown-up surface must match the child's craft.
5. **Device performance pass** — 60fps with all art loaded, cold-start times in
   Release, on the oldest supported hardware.
6. **The store kit** — screenshots and the 15-second ad are how the jury first meets
   you.

Nothing on this list is a redesign. It is all finishing.
